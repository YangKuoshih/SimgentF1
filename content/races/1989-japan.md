The 1989 Japanese Grand Prix at Suzuka decided the World Championship in one of motorsport's most infamous controversies.

• **The Collision**: On Lap 47 of 53, Ayrton Senna lunged down the inside of teammate Alain Prost heading into the Casio Triangle chicane. Prost turned in early, and the two McLaren-Hondas tangled with wheels interlocked, sliding into the escape road.
• **Prost Abandons, Senna Continues**: Prost climbed out assuming the title was won. Senna waved over track marshals for a push-start, fired his Honda engine through the chicane runoff, pitted for a new nosecone, hunted down Alessandro Nannini, and took the chequered flag in P1.
• **Controversial Disqualification**: FISA stewards (headed by French federation president Jean-Marie Balestre) disqualified Senna for 'cutting the chicane' when returning to the track through the escape road. Nannini inherited victory and Prost was crowned 1989 World Champion.
